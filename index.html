2. What is driving the suspensions

The SendGrid backlog

Unsubscribes went from 0.1% to 0.9% on engaged June and July sends to 2% to 5% on the August and September warm-ups. Of the 629 real unsubscribes, 556 (88%) came from contacts getting their first Brevo email.

Buying doesn't protect them

Never-mailed buyers still unsubscribe at 3.1%. Order count, revenue, Brevo's churn score, email domain and signup year barely move the rate.

The SendGrid segments are broken

Segments 24 to 30 contradict each other: 1,775 of the 3,186 contacts in "engaged 90d" are also in "never engaged". Audiences picked from these segments are close to random.

A consent gap

10,727 contacts marked WooCommerce "not-subscribed" have already been mailed, including 1,638 who clicked in the last 90 days. If they opted in somewhere else, record it and move the clickers to SEND.

4. How JEV was used, and what it showed

JEV read every contact: 118,769 subscribed contacts plus 11,276 backtest rows, 3 questions each (unsubscribe risk, whether the inbox is real, value as a buyer). Then we checked its answers against real unsubscribes.

Where JEV helped

  • Every contact, no sampling. All 118,769 rows, as agreed in the meeting, in about 3 minutes for $2.31 a run.
  • Cheap to re-run. Fixing the leaks took a full second run, which cost $2.31. At that price, auditing and re-scoring is easy.
  • The negative result. A clean, full-list test showed per-contact prediction doesn't work on this backlog. That stops the team from spending more time building "engaged" segments out of it.

Where it didn't

  • It doesn't set the tiers. On clean data it ranked unsubscribers no better than chance, so the final list would be the same without it.
  • It trusted what it was shown. In run 1 it picked up a field that only changes after someone unsubscribes. You need a leak audit before trusting any score.
  • The limit is the data, not the model. Most contacts have never been mailed from Brevo, so there is no behaviour for any model to read.

LLM estimates use the token counts we measured: about 48 tokens per contact, 370 tokens of shared context and questions per call, and about 25 output tokens per contact. Prices are Anthropic list prices per million tokens: Haiku 4.5 $1 in / $5 out, Sonnet 5 $2 / $10, Opus 5.5 $4 / $20. This job needed two full runs because of the leak fix, so double every figure: JEV $4.62 against $24 to $188 for the LLM options.

Bottom line on JEV: for reading a full list, it is 5x to 40x cheaper than an LLM and fast enough to re-run after every fix. On this account, though, no method could predict unsubscribes from pre-send data. The tiers come from measured email history, and JEV's value was the fast, cheap, complete test that proved it.

6. What the team should do next

  1. Next campaign: SEND only (748). Add SEND-SMALL in batches of 500 or fewer, and pause if a batch goes over 0.5% unsubscribes.
  2. Leave out NEW-EXCLUDE, NO-CONSENT, SUPPRESS and REPERMISSION-ONLY.
  3. HOLD: one last-chance email, then drop anyone who doesn't engage.
  4. The backlog (76,618): a double opt-in email in batches of 1,000 or fewer, spaced out. Only people who confirm rejoin the normal audience. Expect about 3.5% unsubscribes per batch.
  5. Fix segments 24 to 30 before anyone builds audiences from them again.
  6. Check consent for the 1,638 recent clickers marked WooCommerce "not-subscribed". If they opted in elsewhere, record it and move them to SEND.

7. Method notes

  • Backtest cut-off 1 Aug 2026. Features as of that date; label = unsubscribed or complained afterwards. Contacts created after the cut-off or already unsubscribed before it are left out.
  • Fields hidden from the backtest because they change after an unsubscribe: WooCommerce subscription status, the audience lists #63 and #64, and live engagement segments. All of them would otherwise hand the answer to the model.
  • Brevo only keeps per-contact email history from June 2026, so "never mailed" means never mailed from Brevo in that window.
  • JEV model: typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917 through OpenRouter, 100 contacts per call, 3 questions per contact.
  • Pull: 153,608 per-contact history calls plus about 300 for contacts, lists, segments and campaigns. Campaign recipient exports would cut that to minutes and are the route for the reusable tool.
