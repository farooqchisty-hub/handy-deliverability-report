The short version

  • The suspensions started when the old SendGrid list began getting Brevo emails. First emails lose 3.6% of people.
  • 88% of subscribed contacts have no Brevo email history, so there's nothing to judge them on. We hold them.
  • The 14,189 people Brevo has emailed get a tier from how they actually behaved.
  • Risk falls with every email someone stays for: 3.6% on the first, 0.5% by the fifth.
  • JEV scored everyone in 3 minutes for $2.31, but on no-history contacts it was a coin flip.
  • The way to unlock the held 103,701: export SendGrid's open and click history for list #64.

1. The decision: only tier people we've emailed

You can only predict who'll unsubscribe from people you've seen react to an email. Brevo keeps that history per contact from June onwards. Everyone mailed in that window has a real track record. Everyone else doesn't, and no model or attribute could tell them apart (section 7). So we split the list in two:

Emailed from Brevo: 14,189

Tiered by their own clicks and opens. The rates are measured on what similar people did after 1 Aug. This is the audience for the next campaigns.

Never emailed from Brevo: 103,701

Held. 79,840 of them came from SendGrid, where they were emailed before the move. Their SendGrid history is the missing piece (section 4).

How this changed from earlier versions
  • v1: excluded 113,898, because first emails ran at 3.6%. Too blunt: it treated everyone with no history the same.
  • v2: split the no-history pool into ramp waves using orders, time on list and email provider. It helped a little (0.56 accuracy, 0.5 is a coin flip), but it still meant guessing about people nobody had seen react.
  • v3 (this page): only people with Brevo history get a sendable tier. This also fixes a v2 mistake: 8,727 contacts who had been emailed (WooCommerce buyers without opt-in) were given first-email rules instead of their own history.

4. How to unlock the held pool

79,840 of the held contacts were emailed through SendGrid before the move. SendGrid knows who opened and clicked. Brevo only got the list, and its SendGrid segments (24 to 30) are broken: more than half of "engaged 90 days" is also in "never engaged".

What to ask for

  • A per-contact export for SendGrid list #64 with: last open date, last click date, emails received, bounces, spam reports and unsubscribe date.
  • Usually the Email Activity export or a contact export with engagement fields, depending on the SendGrid plan. Whoever ran the SendGrid account can pull it.

What we'd do with it

  1. Match it to Brevo contacts by email.
  2. Check it predicts: the people Brevo has already emailed from list #64 give us a backtest.
  3. Move recent SendGrid clickers into a slow ramp, and put long-dead contacts (no opens in 12+ months) on the do-not-mail list.
  4. Update UNSUB_RISK_TIER. Rescoring takes minutes once the file lands.

If the export can't be found, there's a fallback: the v2 ramp waves are kept in the contact file (9,768 in wave 1, 54,918 in wave 2, 38,717 excluded). Using them is a business call, because it means emailing people we can't assess.

7. What JEV did, and didn't do

JEV read every subscribed contact: 118,769 rows, 3 questions each (how likely to unsubscribe, is the inbox real, how valuable as a buyer). For 88% of them it had no email behaviour to read, only an address and sometimes some orders.

Where it helped

  • Scored all 118,769 contacts in about 3 minutes for $2.31.
  • Cheap enough to re-run after we fixed the data, which we did.
  • Showed clearly that people with no history can't be predicted, which is what led to holding them.

Where it didn't

  • On clean data it scored 0.54 overall and 0.51 on the no-history group, a coin flip.
  • On the first run it was fooled by a field that changes after someone unsubscribes.
  • It doesn't set any tier. Its score is kept in the CSV as a column.

Bottom line: JEV is 5x to 40x cheaper than an LLM for reading a whole list, and fast enough to re-run after every fix. It just can't find signal that isn't there.

8. The send plan

We don't know Brevo's exact suspension line. What we do know: sends at 0.1% to 0.9% went fine, and sends at 2.5% to 4.9% got rejected. So aim for 1% or less per campaign.

  1. Next campaigns: SEND (2,386) every time, plus SEND-SMALL (3,825) in batches of 1,000 or fewer.
  2. HOLD (7,387): one last-chance email in batches of about 2,000. Anyone who doesn't open within a week gets dropped.
  3. Give it three days: at least three days between emails to the same person.
  4. Stop rule: if a send goes over 1%, pause that tier for the next campaign. Anyone who marks spam comes off right away.
  5. Don't mail HOLD-NO-HISTORY or SUPPRESS, and don't build audiences from segments 24 to 30.
  6. Chase the SendGrid export (section 4). It's the only way to bring most of the held pool back safely.
  7. Confirm consent for the 8,727 emailed contacts who are WooCommerce buyers without opt-in.
Method notes
  • Backtest: features as of 1 Aug 2026; label = unsubscribed or complained after that. 11,276 contacts, 629 unsubscribes. First-email analysis: 15,189 first Brevo emails since June, 542 unsubscribes.
  • Hidden from the backtest because they change after someone unsubscribes: WooCommerce subscription status, lists #63 and #64, and live engagement segments.
  • A signal only counted if it held in both halves of the data (first emails before and after 15 Aug). Orders, time on list and email provider held. Consent, Brevo churn score, RFM, SendGrid clicker and product lists flipped.
  • Brevo keeps per-contact email history from June 2026. The account has 279 campaigns before that, back to 2021, which we can't see per contact.
  • JEV: typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917 via OpenRouter, 100 contacts per call, 3 questions each. Two full runs, $4.62 in total.
  • The pull took about 2.5 hours through the API (153,608 per-contact calls). Campaign recipient exports would cut that to minutes next time.
